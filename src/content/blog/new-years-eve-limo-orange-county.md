For 31 December 2026, nine weeks out is the end of October. Book the vehicle class and the start time then, not the whole itinerary. Large vehicles and the 6pm to 8pm outbound window go first. If you are reading this in December, you are choosing from what is left.

Nine weeks before the date is 29 October. Ten weeks was 22 October, so that half of the window is already behind you. December inquiries start in September and change tone by mid December. People stop asking which vehicle they want and start asking what is left. In most years we are still taking bookings in the final week. The list is just shorter.

## Why December looks different on our calendar

We have run chauffeured cars out of Stanton since 2002. What follows is our own booking calendar across those Decembers, not a survey of the county.

The fleet does not grow in December. Same vehicles in the last week of the year as in the second week of March. A normal month spreads work across 30 nights. December does not. [Corporate holiday party transportation](/corporates/) lands on Thursday, Friday and Saturday, and December 2026 has four Saturdays: 5, 12, 19 and 26. In past Decembers the first three have carried most of our holiday party work, because the 26th lands after Christmas, when most offices are closed. Christmas Day itself is a Friday, 25 December, which pulls a slice of office work forward into the week before it.

Then 31 December lands on a Thursday, and 1 January 2027 is a Friday, so for most offices the next day is closed. That does not shorten the night. It lengthens it.

Demand does not fill a night evenly either. Two waves: outbound between 6pm and 8pm, and the return between 12:15am and 1:30am. A vehicle free at 4pm on 31 December is not available at 7pm, and the gap between a dinner booking and a return is too short to resell. A full fleet can look half empty at four and have nothing at seven.

So book early is incomplete advice. Book early for a block, because the block is what runs out.

## Group sizing, with coats

Every vehicle has a posted capacity. In December, plan on one or two seats fewer than that number. That is a rule of thumb from December bookings, not a limit built into the vehicle, and the party bus is the exception at the bottom of the list.

Coats are the reason. Fourteen people in a Sprinter in July is fourteen people. Fourteen in coats in December is fourteen people and fourteen coats, and the coats need somewhere to sit. They end up on seats, and there is no floor left once the handbags are down. So we quote December groups against a smaller number.

- Suburban SUV: 6 passengers, 6 bags. Five for a night out in December.
- Preferred Chrysler 300: 9. Seven in December.
- Lincoln MKT: 12. Ten in December.
- [SUV stretch](/hummer-limo-service/): 10 to 14 depending on the vehicle. Nine to twelve in December. Ask which one, because 10 and 14 are different nights.
- Sprinter limo: 14, with stand-up headroom. Twelve in December. The headroom earns its money when everyone is in a coat and heels.
- Party bus: 30. Twenty-six in December, four fewer rather than two, because lounge seating and a dance floor take space that bench seating does not.

A group of 13 in a 12-passenger stretch spends the drive apologizing and rearranging knees. Book the count you get in coats, not the count from a planning lunch in September. Gifts count too: a bag per couple that did not exist in June.

On 31 December there is usually nothing left to upgrade into.

## The midnight problem

At 12:05am on 1 January, every chauffeured vehicle in the county is trying to reach the same few blocks.

Not the same building. The same four or five blocks, where the crowds are and where the streets close. Barricades go up and the officer on the corner waves traffic through. There is nowhere for it to stop. Your chauffeur is not parked at the curb with the engine off. He is circling, or double parked and being moved along. Your group comes out at 12:10 with four percent battery and no idea which corner the car is on.

**Pick a fixed point, not a moving one.** Where you were dropped is a moving point, because you moved. A fixed point is an address, a corner, a landmark you can see from a doorway. Two blocks off the main strip usually beats the front door: less traffic, room to stop.

**Send a pin, not a description.** Text it before you go in, not at midnight when nobody has signal. One person holds it. If four people text the chauffeur four different corners, he picks one and three people stand in the wrong place.

**Build 30 minutes into the end.** Leaving at 1am? Contract to 1:30. Otherwise you get the overtime conversation at the curb at 1:05, in the cold, with your group holding opinions. Overtime on 31 December is the worst negotiation of the year, because the car behind you is booked and it is not moving.

Then the headcount. Do it at the door, inside, in the light, before anyone puts a coat on. Give the chauffeur a number and a name. Twelve, and I am the one who counts. Two people will always be in the bathroom, and one is saying goodbye to someone they met forty minutes earlier. Counting at the curb is how twelve becomes nine and a phone call. Nobody leaves the building until the count is right.

## The chauffeur is the part you are buying

What you are paying for at 12:40am is not the lights in the ceiling. It is a person who started a shift in the afternoon, has not been drinking, knows the way back to Huntington Beach at one in the morning, and is standing next to the car when the last two people come out. Our chauffeurs work the night sober and stay with the vehicle. Nobody in your group makes a driving decision at 1am or hunts for parking in Costa Mesa.

## Twenty people: one bus or two vehicles

One bus. The [party bus](/party-bus-rental/) seats 30. Twenty people rattle around in it, which is the right problem in December. One pickup, one drop, one bill, nobody separated at midnight.

Two vehicles. Two [Sprinters](/sprinter-limo/) at 14 each covers 20 with coats. Two addresses, two schedules, two gratuities, and two halves of a group that cannot hear each other.

On the hourly rates operators publish, the bus usually wins on money. Two 14-passenger Sprinters cost more per hour between them than one 30-passenger bus, before the second gratuity. Our [guide to Orange County limo rental costs](/blog/limo-rental-orange-county-cost/) runs that arithmetic on published rates rather than ours.

Two vehicles win in one case. When half the group leaves at 11 and half at 2. One bus forces one schedule. If your 20 people have two bedtimes, price two vehicles and accept the split.

## What to ask on the call

Six questions, five minutes.

- **Deposit.** How much, when it is due, refundable or not. Then the one people forget: if we move the date, does the deposit come with it.
- **Cancellation.** The notice window and what is kept inside it, in the same email as the quote.
- **Overtime on 31 December.** The rate, whether it bills in full hours, and whether another booking sits behind yours. If it does, your end time is real.
- **Whether the rate changes for 31 December.** Ask for a flat figure for your date, in writing. We quote per trip, so that number is the number.
- **Decorations.** What is included, who hangs them, what is banned in that vehicle. Tape and glitter do not come out of every fabric.
- **Who answers the phone at 1am.** The number, whether it is the one you called in October, and whether a person picks up.

An operator who answers all six plainly is worth more than one who answers three. Our [guide to finding a reliable limousine service in Anaheim](/blog/the-honest-guide-to-finding-a-reliable-limousine-service-in-anaheim/) has the longer checklist.

## When a limo is the wrong call

Sometimes the answer is no.

- Five people on a ten-minute hop. A rideshare is cheaper, and a four-hour minimum makes the car a waiting room for three of those hours.
- A hotel with a shuttle. Use the shuttle out and one car for the late return.
- Everyone drives and nobody drinks. Then you are renting upholstery.
- A group that cannot agree on one departure time. That is two cars, not one bus and an argument.
- One venue, eight minutes away, and the night happens inside it. Book the return trip, not six hours.

## Questions we get in December

**How far in advance should I book a limo for New Year's Eve?**

Nine to ten weeks is the comfortable window, which puts late October in the frame for 31 December 2026. Large vehicles and Saturday nights go first. Early December still works, from what is left.

**Is New Year's Eve more expensive for a limo?**

The date sets the rate, so 31 December usually prices above a quiet Tuesday. Ask for a flat figure for your date in writing, and ask whether a holiday minimum sits on top.

**Can we drink in the limo?**

Confirm it at booking, because the rules differ by operator and by vehicle. The legal drinking age in California is 21, and that applies inside the car. Where alcohol is allowed, operators usually attach conditions: what may be brought aboard, whether glass is permitted, whether IDs are checked, and what happens if the conditions are broken. Our vehicles carry ice and stocked refreshments, and the chauffeur drives sober.

**How many people fit in a limo in December?**

Take the posted capacity and subtract one or two seats. Coats and handbags take the rest. A 12-passenger stretch is a 10-person ride in December, a 14-passenger Sprinter is 12, a six-passenger SUV is five. On the 30-passenger party bus, plan on 26.

**What time should the limo pick us up on New Year's Eve?**

Work backwards from the booking, not forwards from the party. The 6pm to 8pm outbound window is the busiest of the night, so an earlier or later start is easier to find. Build the return around 12:30am.

**Can the limo wait while we are inside?**

Yes. The meter runs while the car waits, so decide in advance. On 31 December ask what sits behind your slot, because if anything does, the vehicle cannot extend.
