Book on the number of people who can ride comfortably, not the seated capacity on the spec sheet. A 12-passenger stretch is comfortable for about nine. Choose by headcount, luggage and access, then confirm the exact vehicle with our dispatch team before you pay a deposit.

## The seated number is a spec sheet number

Every vehicle has a published capacity. Ours are on the fleet pages. They are accurate, and they are the least useful number in the booking, because a 12-passenger stretch seats 12 people who are sitting perfectly still, facing forward, with nothing in their hands.

Nobody travels like that.

By 7pm on a Friday your group has coats, because the marine layer usually rolls in off the coast by then and nobody wants to carry a jacket all night. Someone has a camera bag. Someone else is holding a birthday gift they refuse to leave in a trunk. If there is a bride in the car, she needs the seat next to her empty so the dress does not get crushed, which means you just gave up a seat to a dress.

Children are the other one. They count as people, and they arrive with a bag, a blanket and a need to move.

So the number to book on is the seated number minus the space your group actually uses. That second number is what the chart below gives you, and it is a judgement about how the vehicle gets used, not a manufacturer's figure.

"Night out" here means a four hour booking, people in good clothes, phones in hands and a stop or two. If your group is a wedding party with garment bags, take one off each number.

The ratio is not identical across the fleet. The Sprinter is the exception. Stand-up headroom and lounge seating mean 12 of 14 still leaves room to move, while 12 of 12 in a stretch does not. That is why the group table below sends twelve people to the Sprinter, not to the stretch.

Capacity can vary by the individual vehicle. Two vehicles of the same model can differ by a seat depending on how the interior was built, so confirm the exact unit at booking. The numbers above are the fleet standard we quote against.

Read the 10 to 12 row twice. Twelve people who book the 12-passenger stretch is the single most common mistake we correct on the phone. Twelve in a stretch is 100% of capacity. Twelve in the Sprinter is 86% of capacity with headroom to stand up in, so the Sprinter is the right car for a group of 12 even though the stretch is the one with the magic number.

## The between-sizes problem

Real groups are rarely a published capacity. They are 13, 20 or 31, and the chart does not have a row for that.

**A group of 13 on a 12-passenger stretch.** Someone is going to suggest that the 13th person sits on a lap, or stands for the short ride. Do not do it. The honest options are the [Sprinter limo](/sprinter-limo/), which seats 14, or two vehicles. The Sprinter works for 13 when nobody has a suitcase and nobody needs to sit sideways. If half the group is flying in with bags, or it is a wedding party with dresses, take two cars or step up to the bus. Thirteen in a Sprinter is one spare seat, not a comfortable ride.

**A group of 20.** The [party bus](/party-bus-rental/) is usually the honest answer, because 20 sits well under the 24 it carries comfortably and one vehicle means one pickup, one drop-off and one chauffeur keeping the night on schedule. The alternative is two Sprinters, which makes sense when the group is quiet, older, or going somewhere a bus cannot load. Two Sprinters for 20 also leaves room for the friend who always brings a plus one.

**A group of 31.** Thirty one does not fit in a 30-seat bus. There is no clever seating arrangement that changes that. You are booking a party bus plus a second vehicle, and the second vehicle can be a Suburban or a Sprinter depending on how the group splits.

## Luggage is the number people forget

Airport runs are a different problem from a night out, and the vehicle that fits the people often does not fit the bags.

Only one vehicle in the fleet has a published bag figure: the Suburban carries six. Everything else should be sized by dispatch against your actual bag count, because trunk shape matters as much as trunk size. Hard cases, golf clubs and strollers take space differently than duffels do, and a number on a spec sheet will not tell you which.

So do this instead. Count the bags. Say whether they are checked cases, carry-ons, garment bags or something odd like a surfboard. Then let dispatch pick. The [black car service page](/black-car-service/) covers the Suburban and the airport work it does.

If you are running a group to LAX or John Wayne with luggage and a tight flight, the bag count decides the vehicle, not the headcount.

## If the vehicle can't reach the door

A vehicle that cannot reach the door is the wrong vehicle, however many seats it has. This is the part nobody asks about until the night of.

A stretch limo is long and low. A steep driveway can scrape the bumper or leave the car high centered. Gated communities add a call box, a code and a wait. On Newport Coast and in the hills above Laguna Beach, the driveway can be steeper than the street it comes off. Residential streets with cars parked on both sides can make the turn into a cul-de-sac impossible in a 30-seat bus. Some venues give you one loading spot with a ten minute window, and a bus takes longer to empty than a Suburban.

None of that is a reason to skip the bigger vehicle. It is a reason to say something when you book. Tell dispatch the pickup address, whether the street is narrow or sloped, whether there is a gate, and where the venue wants vehicles to load. If the answer is that the bus cannot get up the drive, we meet you at the corner. That is a two minute walk and no drama.

## One vehicle or two

Every vehicle carries its own hourly minimum, its own chauffeur and its own pickup window. Two vehicles are not twice the capacity for the same money. They are roughly twice the booking.

That does not make two vehicles wrong. It makes the comparison the wrong one. Divide the quote by the number of people and look at the per-person figure.

A bus looks expensive until you divide it by 24. Two limos look reasonable until you divide by 12 and add the second pickup, the second set of stops and the second driver waiting outside the restaurant. For a group of 16, one bus almost always wins on cost per person. For a group of 9 going to dinner and back, two cars is a waste of money.

## What to tell dispatch

Five facts produce a correct quote. Leave one out and you get a guess.

1. **Headcount**, split into adults and children.
2. **Bag count and type.** Checked cases, carry-ons, garment bags, golf clubs, strollers.
3. **Every address**, plus gate codes, loading zone instructions and whether the street is narrow or steep.
4. **Date, pickup time and hours.** Including the return, if you want the car to wait.
5. **The occasion and who is riding.** A bride, a grandparent, a group in heels, a group of 20 year olds.

Most bookings that go sideways start with a headcount that was two people optimistic. The customer is not lying. They counted the people who said yes in the group chat, and then four more showed up.

## Frequently asked questions

### How many people fit in a 12 passenger limo?

Twelve people fit in a 12-passenger stretch if everyone sits still and travels light. For a night out with coats, bags and a couple of stops, plan on nine. Groups of 10 to 14 should look at the Sprinter, which seats 14.

### What size limo do I need for 15 people?

Fifteen people do not fit in one Sprinter, which seats 14. Book the 30-seat party bus, or split into two vehicles if the group is quiet or the route is tight. For most groups of 15, the party bus is the simpler answer.

### Is a party bus cheaper than two limos?

Often it is cheaper per person. A party bus carries up to 30 seated and runs on one chauffeur, one hourly minimum and one pickup, while two limos double the minimum and the stops. Divide each quote by your headcount and compare the per-person figure.

### Can a limo fit in a normal driveway?

Sometimes. A stretch limo is long and low, so a steep driveway can scrape it and a narrow street with parked cars can block the turn. Tell dispatch the address and the slope, and the chauffeur can meet you at the curb instead.

### Do children count toward limo capacity?

Yes. Every person in the vehicle counts toward capacity, including infants and toddlers, and children usually travel with more gear. Give dispatch the number of adults and the number of children when you book.

### What if our group is one or two people over?

Move up a vehicle or take two. A 13th person on a 12-passenger stretch means someone is standing or sitting on a lap, which is not a plan for a night out. Tell dispatch the real count and they will size it.
