## What a limo rental rate actually buys

Comparing hourly rates as though hours were equal is the usual mistake. A six-hour Saturday wedding and a four-hour Tuesday transfer are different products wearing the same unit of measure.

The three bookings below use the published numbers, gratuity at 20%, no overtime, no extra stops, no cleaning fees. None of them is a quote.

### A 6-hour Saturday wedding, Chrysler 300 stretch, 9 passengers

The C-300 limousine is published at $125 an hour. A Saturday [wedding](/wedding/) is the booking people research hardest and commit to last.

6 x $125 = $750 before gratuity.
Gratuity: $750 x 0.20 = $150.
Total: $900.

A nine-passenger job cannot use the $90 vehicles, because the sedan seats three and the Suburban seats six. The real band for this trip runs from the Lincoln MKT stretch at $100 to the Hummer at $150:

Low: 6 x $100 = $600, plus $120 gratuity = $720.
High: 6 x $150 = $900, plus $180 gratuity = $1,080.

Same six hours, $720 to $1,080 depending on the operator, the date, and whether gratuity sits inside the quote or gets added at the end. On the $750 booking, that last detail is worth $150.

Overtime is the other lever. One extra hour at $125 is $125 before gratuity, and contracts usually bill in full-hour blocks. A wedding running twenty minutes late can cost the full hour.

### A 5-hour Friday prom, Hummer for 14

Hummer and stretch SUV, $150 an hour, 10 to 14 passengers.

5 x $150 = $750.
Gratuity: $750 x 0.20 = $150.
Total: $900.

Fourteen people fit one Hummer. The alternative is two Lincoln MKT stretches at $100 an hour, which seat 9 each.

2 vehicles x 5 hours x $100 = $1,000 before gratuity.
Gratuity: $1,000 x 0.20 = $200.
Total: $1,200.

Splitting costs $300 more, a third again, and puts the group in two cars that cannot talk to each other between stops. That usually ends the discussion. Worth asking in April or May: does the minimum change for prom weekends? It often does.

### A 4-hour bachelorette party bus for 22

Party buses are published at $100 to $200 an hour on genesislimoservice.com, which says the number moves with vehicle type, group size and time of year. The 45-passenger bus carries $200 an hour on wantlimo.com, plus a separate $200 bathroom fee.

Low, for a smaller bus at the genesislimoservice.com floor: 4 x $100 = $400, plus $80 gratuity = $480.
High, for the 45-passenger bus at the wantlimo.com rate: 4 x $200 = $800, plus $160 gratuity = $960.
The $200 bathroom fee is printed against that 45-passenger bus, so it attaches to the high figure only: $960 + $200 = $1,160.

Across 22 people that is $21.82 a head at the low end and $52.73 at the high end, which is the range you are actually shopping.

Twenty-two people do not need the 45-passenger bus that carries the $200 rate. Expect the middle of that band, not the top. The $100 end is real, but it is rarely the vehicle you want on a Saturday night in June. Most [party bus rentals](/party-bus-rental/) price that way: vehicle first, date second.

None of those totals is a quote. They are the shape of one, and your date and address list turn the shape into a number.

## What moves the number

- Vehicle class. $90 an hour for a sedan, $200 for a 45-passenger bus. Pick the smallest vehicle the group fits in comfortably.
- Minimum hours. Four is the common floor, and wantlimo.com states it in its FAQ. A three-hour trip still bills four.
- Day and season. Saturday over Wednesday. Prom season, wedding season and December over February.
- Distance and stops. Hourly pricing buys time, not miles. Ask about a mileage cap or fuel surcharge on longer runs.
- Wait time. The meter runs while the car sits, unless the operator prices waiting separately. Ask which.
- Headcount versus bags. Six people with six bags is the Suburban spec. Six with twelve bags is a [Sprinter limo](/sprinter-limo/). Guess wrong and you upgrade on the day at whatever the rate is then.
- Gratuity. 15% to 20% is the usual ask, and these examples use 20%. Whether it sits inside the quoted total or lands at the end changes everything.
- Fees. Fuel, airport, admin, cleaning, bathroom, damage. Two were printed on the pages above. The rest arrive later.
- Deposits. Usually a card to hold the date. genesislimoservice.com states that its deposit is non-refundable. Ask for the cancellation window next to it.

## Five questions that tell you if a quote is real

**Is gratuity in this number?** A straight answer is "yes, 20% included" or "no, add 20%". "The chauffeur takes care of himself" means 20% is coming out of your pocket at the end. On the wedding above, this question is worth $150.

**What is the minimum, and am I above it?** Ask how many hours you are billed, not how many you will use. Three hours wanted, four minimum, you pay four.

**What is the overtime rate, and how is it billed?** A good answer is a figure and a rule, like "$125 an hour, full-hour blocks, chauffeur's call." The bad one is "we'll work it out." Overtime is where a good night picks up a bill nobody quoted.

**What happens if my flight lands two hours late?** This decides airport work. A flat-rate operator tracks the flight and moves the pickup without re-pricing. An hourly operator can bill the wait. Ask about the grace period. Thirty minutes is common.

**What do I get back if I cancel, and where is that written?** Get the terms in the same email as the quote. "Non-refundable" is a complete answer and survivable if you know it. "We're flexible" is not an answer. Weather and illness happen, and the policy decides who eats it.

Five questions, five minutes on the phone. An operator who answers all five in plain numbers is worth twenty dollars an hour more than one who dodges three.

## The traps

**A headline rate that excludes everything.** Say a quote shows $75 an hour. Six hours is $450. Add 20% gratuity at $90 and a $40 fuel and admin charge and the same six hours is $580, which is $96.67 an hour. Compare totals, never rates.

**Per-person pricing that flips at four passengers.** Six people at $60 a head is $360. Four hours in a Suburban at $90 an hour is $360, and the car seats six with room for the bags. Above four passengers, ask what the private vehicle costs instead.

**A quote with no CPUC number.** Charter work in California requires TCP authority, and the number has to appear in advertising and on the vehicle. If a company cannot produce one, you have no way to tell what coverage sits behind the car.

**Non-refundable deposits with no written terms.** A deposit with terms attached is normal business. A deposit with no terms anywhere leaves you nothing to point at.

**"Starting at" rates for a car that is never free on a Saturday.** A $99 an hour landing page usually belongs to a sedan, midweek, in a slow month. The rate is real for that vehicle on that date. It is not your booking. Ask what that vehicle costs on your date.

## When hourly is the wrong model

Airport transfers price per trip. John Wayne to Anaheim is a fixed run: car, chauffeur, bags, done. Point-to-point work prices the same way, hotel to venue, venue to restaurant.

Run the alternative. A four-hour minimum at the low end is 4 x $90 = $360, and $432 with 20% gratuity. A single transfer priced per trip comes in below that unless the distance is long, and the four hours do not get you there faster. They keep the car parked. Our [airport transportation service](/airport-transportation-service/) prices by the run for that reason, and the [LAX to Disneyland guide](/blog/lax-to-disneyland-limo-service/) shows how those fares get built.

Hourly is right when the car stays with you: a wedding with photos between ceremony and reception, a winery loop, [prom](/prom/), a concert with a 1am pickup. If the car will sit in a lot for two of the four hours, you are buying a parked car.

## Check the operator is licensed

California requires charter-party carriers to hold TCP authority from the CPUC, and the TCP number has to appear in advertising and on the vehicle. Ask for it before you discuss the rate. It should take seconds to produce.

Carriers file liability coverage under CPUC General Order 115 and operate under GO 157-E. Limousines carry CPUC and DMV special plates under Public Utilities Code 5385.6.

Check authority at the [CPUC TCP licensing page](https://www.cpuc.ca.gov/tcpinfo/). The GO 115 text sits on the [CPUC document server](https://docs.cpuc.ca.gov/PublishedDocs/Published/G000/M322/K150/322150628.pdf). There is a longer checklist in our guide to [finding a reliable limousine service in Anaheim](/blog/the-honest-guide-to-finding-a-reliable-limousine-service-in-anaheim/), including what to ask about insurance.
